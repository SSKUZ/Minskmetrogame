A stop-level policy controls demand inside the existing simulation. Platforms
contain 80–100 people over the four-car length. Most wait behind the approach
lanes; only selected intact groups approach. Ordinary demand is 30–34 across
the entire train, balanced by car, with reductions toward the end or at the
target load. Existing models, animations, LOD, grips and movement are retained.

Seat/standing anchors determine capacity, leaving circulation space. Peaks are
80 riders on the eight-door temporary stand and 108 on the sixteen-door contract
fixture. Seats are preferred by about 80% of selected people, with existing family
seating and standing fallbacks. No new crowd or animation architecture is added.
Targets rise, plateau in the middle, then decline. Every remaining rider at the
terminal uses an ordinary animated exit; no terminal boarding or train-space
despawning is used. The completed zero-onboard state exposes readyForNextTrip.

Corrected small existing navigation/queue defects exposed by the new profile:
- A follower cannot occupy a bay still present in the active boarder's path.
- An obsolete detour corner can be dropped when the swept direct route is clear.
- Nonboarding people yield occupied side queue bays, not just central exit lanes.
- Exit groups are selected near different available doors, reducing sequential
  long walks along the aisle. This is stop planning, not a new crowd simulator.
