## Implemented

- 80–100 platform occupants distributed along the four-car length. Most wait
  behind the boarding lanes; the existing models and animations are retained.
- Ordinary demand is 30–34 people across the entire train (inside the requested
  30–40 range), with balanced car quotas and intact groups. Admission decreases
  late in the route or at the target occupancy. Platform crowd size and actual
  boardings are separate values.
- A small stop-level policy sets a rising, middle-peak and declining load curve.
  Safe seat/standing anchors determine capacity, with circulation reserve and
  a browser-oriented cap. Existing queues, navigation and animation are reused.
- The temporary stand peaks at 80 riders (20/car); the four-door-per-car contract
  peaks at 108 (27/car). Seat preference uses existing sitting/standing behavior.
- Alighting groups use nearby different doors. Nonboarding people yield side
  queue bays as well as exit lanes. An active boarder retains its pending bay;
  an obsolete detour corner can be skipped only after a swept clear-route check.
- At the terminal every remaining rider is scheduled for normal animated exit,
  new boarding is disabled, and the completion signals must both be true.
  No inside-train despawning, teleportation or emergency recovery is used.
- readyForNextTrip becomes true only for a completed safe terminal exchange with
  zero riders. A subsequent route starts with the empty existing train.

## Timing and remaining limits

The sixteen-door contract completes ordinary exchanges in 7.27–25.27 s, within
the requested 20–35 s upper target; quiet stops can finish sooner. Terminal
unloading takes 11.97 s. Its ordinary acceptance ceiling is 35 s.

The unchanged eight-door stand is slower: ordinary stops take 19.67–49.23 s,
terminal unloading 26.50 s. Its 60 s functional ceiling is explicitly NOT the
20–35 s timing criterion. Busy eight-door stops do not meet that target. This
limitation is retained, not hidden by faster walking, fewer platform people,
family splitting, timer-based rejection or changing carriage geometry.

The game's render/viewport checks do not certify FPS on physical Android
hardware. Existing LOD and resource management are retained; no new LOD system,
models, animations or complex crowd mechanics are added.
